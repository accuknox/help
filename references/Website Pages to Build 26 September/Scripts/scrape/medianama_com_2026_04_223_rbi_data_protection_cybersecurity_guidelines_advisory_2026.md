# Lowdown: RBI’s new advisory pushes for tighter data use, AI safeguards, vendor checks

The Cyber Security and IT Risk Group of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has issued an advisory, directing all regulated entities to prioritise customer data protection. The move aligns with the [**Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023**(DPDPA)](https://www.medianama.com/2023/08/223-summary-india-digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023/). While the DPDPA lays down the legal framework for data protection, the RBI’s **advisory provides sector-specific guidelines** by detailing how **data minimisation, user consent, data erasure and platform accountability** would work in the financial ecosystem.

**1\. Governance and regulatory compliance**

- Banks, fintechs, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), and payment aggregators will now be required to get formal **approval at appropriate governance levels for any policies and frameworks related to customer data security, privacy and third-party risks.**
- They must conduct **board- or board-designated committee-level meetings** to review data **security risks and incidents** on a “ **quarterly or semi-annual**” basis.
- **Entities are required to** **define clear roles and responsibilities for customer data protection** (e.g., CISO, Data Protection Officer, business owners) and require regular reporting of metrics, exceptions, and audit findings to senior management and the Board/Committee.
- They must use RACI (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) accountability frameworks for **clarity in ownership for governance, monitoring, and incident reporting**.
- A steering committee or **cross-functional oversight mechanism** must be set up to periodically oversee customer data governance and associated risks.

**2\. Data collection, classification and usage**

- **Entities should use automated tools for data tagging and classification**. These tools will identify, label and store customer data based on their sensitivity across in-house systems, cloud networks and third-party systems.
- They must implement centralised platforms or mechanisms for capturing, tracking, and updating user consent.
- **Entities are required to communicate data collection and privacy policy to users at key interaction points, such as during onboarding, account setup and transactions**. Customers must be informed about what data is collected, how it is used, and their rights regarding consent and data usage.

**3\. Data retention and destruction**

- The advisory requires regulated entities to create a **clear policy documenting how long customer and non-customer data (e.g., potential clients who have applied but are not onboarded) is retained and when it is deleted across live systems, testing environments, and backups.**
- Entities must conduct periodic reviews to enforce retention timelines and ensure proper deletion, while maintaining audit trails for all data deletion and modification activities.
- They should use standard destruction methods such as secure wiping, cryptographic erasure, or physical destruction, adhering to NIST 800-88 or equivalent standards. India’s **DPDPA mandates the erasure of personal data once the purpose of collection is fulfilled. But India has no domestic technical standard specifying how that erasure must be carried out.** In the absence of such a standard, the RBI has pointed regulated entities toward an US framework, a gap that India’s [Data Protection Board](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-dpdp-act-data-protection-board-priorities/), which operationalised last year, will need to address.

**4\. Third-party data sharing and purpose limitation**

- As per the advisory, **regulated entities can share user data with third parties only for necessary and defined purposes**. They must review their existing data- **sharing arrangements with third party vendors**, and implement appropriate safeguards, such as anonymisation and pseudonymisation of data in order to reduce exposure risks.
- Entities are required to conduct due diligence on third-party vendors before onboarding, including background checks. Post-onboarding, periodic reviews must be conducted to assess how vendors use customer data in day-to-day operations.
- **They must restrict the storage of sensitive customer data in plain text by third parties.**
- Implement contractual arrangements that **require third-party vendors to promptly report data breaches or security incidents related to customer data**.
- **Entities must also conduct periodic cyber drills and involve third parties to identify blind spots**, test cyber resilience, and improve coordination under stress.

**5\. AI and data risks**

> Mitigating data exposure risks for emerging technologies requires a comprehensive framework with layered security controls and continuous monitoring,” the RBI said.

It has directed all regulated entities to adopt the following practices:

- Implement controls such as **API gateways, rate limiting, logging, threat detection and secure hosting of customer data when AI systems or chatbots handle customer data-related** use cases. Risk mitigation measures must be proportionate to the use case and associated risks.
- Enable continuous monitoring and risk detection through SIEM systems, API monitoring, anomaly detection, and, where applicable, AI model performance evaluation, along with proactive identification and mitigation of security risks.

**6\. Grievance redressal mechanism**

- Entities must deploy a CRM system that generates **unique complaint reference numbers and sends automated alerts** to customers upon complaint registration and closure.
- They are required to establish a **multi-channel grievance redressal framework**, including email, toll-free numbers, written communication, and in-person support.
- A published escalation matrix with hierarchy, turnaround times, and communication protocols is also listed as a requirement for regulated entities.

**7\. Audit of data security:**

- Customer data security must be included within the scope of internal and information systems (IS) audits, covering control effectiveness and vulnerabilities (e.g., vulnerability assessment and penetration testing), with timely remediation.
- Entities must ensure secure audit log capture, centralisation, and retention with tamper-proof trails, encryption, role-based access controls (RBAC), and regular review.
- Audit processes must also include a review of third-party cybersecurity and data protection measures.

The advisory comes against the backdrop of the RBI’s Payments Vision Report 2028, which noted that the central bank is [examining the possibility of bringing e-commerce platforms under its regulatory ambit](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-rbi-payments-ecosystem-vision-2028-ecommerce-regulation/).

In February 2026 alone, online marketplaces facilitated **114.52 million Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions**, aggregating to Rs 6,461.56 crore in value, according to the latest [data](https://www.npci.org.in/product/ecosystem-statistics/upi) from the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI).
