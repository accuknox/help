Journey Method

How to read this page

The market comparison below uses public UX evidence to understand how each vendor presents onboarding, first value, risk investigation, compliance proof, and remediation. The proposed AccuKnox journey then goes much deeper: where the journey starts, what each screen should look like, what the user does next, what decision is being made, and what the exit state must be.

Core stages used here: 1 Onboarding, 2 First Dashboard, 3 Risk Triage, 4 Asset Detail, 5 Access Graph, 6 Compliance Cockpit, 7 Remediation, 8 Executive Reporting.

Product Onboarding + First Insight Discovery + Risk + Lineage Compliance + Monitoring + Remediation + Reporting

Stage 1

Connector setup and scan readiness

Where the journey starts

Trigger: A cloud security architect connects AWS, GCP, Snowflake, BigQuery, M365, or a SaaS source during evaluation or rollout.

How the screen looks: A four-step wizard with a left-side progress rail, center setup forms, and a right-side confidence panel that shows required IAM scopes, validation status, estimated scan time, and expected first findings.

First visible modules: Environment presets, connector cards, permissions checklist, scan estimate, and a "Why these permissions are needed" explainer.

Primary CTA: Start discovery scan.

Decision point: Is the environment connected with enough permissions to produce trustworthy results?

Exit state: Scan starts successfully and the user sees when the first dashboard will be populated.

Journey prompt for design

Design a light-theme enterprise connector setup wizard for AccuKnox DSPM.
Step 1 selects environment type with AWS, GCP, Azure, Snowflake, BigQuery, and SaaS presets.
Step 2 shows IAM requirements with pass or fail validation chips.
Step 3 shows scope selection and an estimated time-to-insight panel.
Step 4 confirms scan launch and previews the first dashboard modules the user will see.
The screen should feel safer and more explicit than a generic onboarding form. Borrow the clarity of Cyera onboarding, but add AccuKnox-specific cloud-native trust cues.
Stage 2

First dashboard and posture comprehension

Trigger: The first scan completes or a daily review begins.

How the screen looks: A top KPI ribbon, left-side risk trend chart, central sensitive-data posture panel, right-side urgent actions rail, and a lower band for compliance status by RBI, DPDP, TRAI, GDPR, and PCI.

First visible modules: Overall risk score, crown-jewel assets, newly exposed data stores, most over-permissioned identities, critical regulatory gaps, and pending remediation actions.

Primary CTA: View critical risks.

Decision point: Which domain, asset class, or identity pattern deserves first investigation?

Exit state: User opens a filtered triage board that preserves dashboard context.

Journey prompt for design

Create an AccuKnox DSPM landing dashboard in a light theme.
Top row: overall risk score, exposed sensitive assets, over-permissioned identities, unresolved critical findings.
Middle: trend chart on the left, business-unit risk heatmap in the center, urgent actions and SLA rail on the right.
Bottom: compliance posture cards with RBI, DPDP, TRAI, GDPR, PCI and evidence completeness percentages.
The screen should combine the legibility of Sentra's compliance posture and Cyera's dashboard density, while feeling more cloud-native and action-oriented.
Stage 3

Risk triage board

Trigger: User clicks into critical findings from the dashboard or arrives from an alert.

How the screen looks: Filter rail on the left, ranked findings table in the center, explainability side panel on the right.

First visible modules: Severity, sensitivity type, public exposure, access count, cloud account, business owner, regulator impact, and recommended next action.

Primary CTA: Investigate finding.

Decision point: Is this issue urgent because of data sensitivity, access breadth, exploitability, or regulator impact?

Exit state: User opens a single finding with asset detail, lineage, access context, and remediation options.

Journey prompt for design

Design a ranked risk triage board for AccuKnox DSPM.
Use a dense enterprise data table with filters for cloud, data type, regulator impact, business owner, sensitivity, public exposure, and identity risk.
Each finding row should include a short explanation of why it is prioritized.
The right-side drawer should show a summary: what data is at risk, who can access it, what changed recently, and the top recommended fix.
The goal is to reduce scanner noise and make the priority model transparent.
Stage 4

Asset detail and lineage explorer

Trigger: Analyst opens a finding that affects a single dataset, bucket, schema, or file collection.

How the screen looks: Header summary at the top, metadata and ownership in the left column, lineage visualization in the center, historical events and evidence on the right.

First visible modules: Asset identifiers, sensitivity labels, owner, business domain, access count, exposure history, lineage graph, and policy violations.

Primary CTA: Open access path.

Decision point: Is the risk caused by the asset itself, an access path, a cloud misconfiguration, or a combination?

Exit state: User isolates the cause and pivots to the access graph or remediation center.

Journey prompt for design

Design an asset detail page for AccuKnox DSPM that blends Cyera Access Trail concepts with AccuKnox's cloud-native posture context.
Top summary bar: asset name, owner, criticality, cloud account, sensitivity score, open findings.
Main canvas: lineage and movement path of the data.
Right rail: recent changes, compliance impacts, and evidence snippets.
Bottom: related findings and remediation history.
The page should feel investigation-ready, not merely descriptive.
Stage 5

Access graph and blast-radius analysis

Trigger: User needs to understand identity-driven exposure behind the finding.

How the screen looks: Interactive graph canvas in the center, path inspector on the right, identity summary panel on the left, timeline strip at the bottom.

First visible modules: Asset node, identity nodes, groups, roles, inherited permissions, external links, and blast-radius estimate.

Primary CTA: Rightsize access.

Decision point: Which permission edge, group membership, or external share is causing the greatest unnecessary exposure?

Exit state: User selects a path and sends it to remediation with context preserved.

Journey prompt for design

Create an access graph for AccuKnox DSPM in a light enterprise theme.
Show sensitive data assets, human identities, service accounts, groups, inherited roles, and public or external access as distinct node types.
Support hover details, blast-radius count, and an action drawer that suggests rightsizing, exception creation, or owner review.
The graph should be easier to read than a generic network diagram and should clearly connect access paths to regulator impact and data sensitivity.
Stage 6

RBI, DPDP, and TRAI compliance cockpit

Trigger: Compliance review, audit request, internal control failure, or executive ask.

How the screen looks: Control scorecards on top, obligation heatmap in the middle, evidence drawer on the right, failing-asset list below.

First visible modules: Regulator tabs, pass or fail counts, control mappings, missing evidence, open exceptions, and last attestation timestamps.

Primary CTA: Generate evidence pack.

Decision point: Is this a control design issue, an execution gap, or a missing-evidence problem?

Exit state: User exports evidence, opens the failing assets, or routes remediation tasks.

Journey prompt for design

Design a compliance cockpit for AccuKnox DSPM focused on RBI, DPDP, and TRAI, with optional global frameworks as secondary tabs.
Top row: regulator scorecards and evidence completeness percentages.
Center: control heatmap with pass, warning, fail states.
Right rail: evidence drawer showing logs, screenshots, policy artifacts, and remediation status.
Bottom: failing assets and assigned owners.
The screen must feel usable for a real audit meeting, not like a marketing infographic.
Stage 7

Remediation command center

Trigger: A fix is approved, or the analyst wants to open a workflow for rightsizing, access review, quarantine, or policy hardening.

How the screen looks: Queue of proposed actions on the left, detailed playbook pane in the center, approvals and audit log on the right.

First visible modules: Risk reduction estimate, owner assignment, playbook type, automation safety checks, affected assets, and rollback guidance.

Primary CTA: Apply fix, create ticket, or request approval.

Decision point: Should the fix be automated now, routed for approval, or staged with an exception?

Exit state: Remediation becomes an auditable action with clear status and proof of closure.

Journey prompt for design

Create a remediation command center for AccuKnox DSPM.
Left: remediation queue grouped by urgency and playbook type.
Center: detailed fix playbook with affected systems, estimated blast-radius reduction, and execution preview.
Right: approvals, audit log, ticket integrations, and rollback status.
The UX should feel safer and more controllable than a simple "fix now" button.
Stage 8

Executive reporting and sponsor communication

Trigger: Weekly review, steering committee, board prep, or customer champion circulation.

How the screen looks: Narrative scorecards on top, trend visuals in the center, business-unit summaries below, scheduled report settings on the right.

First visible modules: Overall risk reduction, top resolved issues, unresolved criticals, compliance progress, and remediation throughput.

Primary CTA: Schedule weekly report.

Decision point: Is the organization demonstrably safer this week than last week?

Exit state: A sponsor-ready report is exported or delivered automatically to defined stakeholders.

Journey prompt for design

Design a CISO summary screen for AccuKnox DSPM in a light, premium, board-ready visual style.
Include risk trend, critical issues resolved, regulator readiness movement, business-unit comparison, and a short narrative insights panel.
The screen must look credible for a sponsor meeting while preserving drilldown access for analysts.
