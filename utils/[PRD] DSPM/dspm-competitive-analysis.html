Category Thesis

The market has moved from DSPM visibility to DSPM evidence plus action.

Every serious DSPM vendor now tells roughly the same left-to-right story: connect data stores, discover sensitive data, classify, understand who can touch it, prioritize risk, trigger remediation, and prove compliance. The visible battleground is no longer feature coverage. It is proof density. The winning experience shows the fastest path from first scan to a risk-reduction decision that a CISO, architect, and auditor can all understand in under five minutes.

Sentra, Cyera, Varonis, and BigID all invest heavily in outcome framing, visual evidence, and analyst-ready workflows. AccuKnox has a stronger technical right to win than its current DSPM presentation suggests, especially where CNAPP context, runtime security, cloud posture, and Kubernetes-native visibility can be stitched into one operating model. The product gap is therefore not category relevance. It is experience packaging, screenshot proof, and a thinner narrative around regulator-grade evidence and executive-ready dashboards.

Best-in-class DSPM UX is not "here are 1,000 findings." It is "here are the 12 issues that matter, here is why they matter, here is how to fix them, and here is the audit evidence that proves the fix happened."

What Changed

What changed in the DSPM landscape in the last cycle

1. Data security is now sold with AI risk attached

Sentra talks about AI data readiness and preventing copilot data catastrophes. Cyera pushes AI Guardian, Browser Shield, and MCP. Varonis now frames AI security and AI governance alongside access governance. BigID pushes AI governance, AI TRiSM, AI agents, and identity-aware control. The category is no longer only about S3 buckets, Snowflake tables, and M365 files. It is about whether AI tools, copilots, agents, and shadow AI can reach sensitive data with the wrong context and the wrong permissions.

2. Access and blast radius have become board-level UX objects

Cyera Access Trail, Varonis permissions analysis, and BigID access governance all elevate the question of who can reach what. The user experience is shifting from passive catalogs toward attack-path-style reasoning: which identities, groups, external links, and overly broad access paths create material exposure right now.

3. Compliance is presented as a live operating surface

Sentra's compliance posture views, Varonis compliance management pages, BigID privacy plus governance stack, and Cyera's buyer-guide-based risk framing all treat compliance as continuously evidenced rather than a static report pack. The UX lesson is that compliance screens must answer current status, failing controls, impacted data assets, and remediation ownership in one place.

4. Buyers expect quantified time-to-value claims

Sentra publishes scale, accuracy, and cost comparisons. Cyera publishes rapid deployment, precision, and customer outcomes. Varonis publishes blast-radius reduction and managed outcomes. BigID publishes classifier breadth and automation depth. AccuKnox already has metrics on its site, but they are not yet anchored to a clear sequence of user-visible screens and workflow moments.

DSPM Expectations

DSPM Coverage Expansion under Concentric AI (PayNet)

Objective: extend DSPM capabilities beyond Microsoft 365 to AWS environments (starting with S3 buckets), enabling end-to-end visibility, classification, and risk monitoring of sensitive data across PayNet's cloud footprint.

1. Objective and Requirements

Data discovery and classification

  • Automated discovery of structured and unstructured data.
  • Classification of sensitive data (PII, financial data, credentials, etc.).
  • Coverage: Microsoft 365 (existing) and AWS S3 (Phase 2 scope).

Data risk identification

  • Detect over-permissive access, public exposure, and sensitive data in inappropriate locations.
  • Risk scoring based on sensitivity, access exposure, and data location.

Access and governance visibility

  • Identify data owners, access rights, and sharing patterns.
  • Support least-privilege access review.

GenAI security capability

  • Monitor user interaction with GenAI tools (e.g. ChatGPT, web-based AI).
  • Detect sensitive data inputs into AI prompts or uploads.
  • At minimum: visibility and alerting; enforcement if available.

2. Coverage Expansion Requirements

Phase 1 (existing)

  • OneDrive.
  • SharePoint.

Phase 2 (expansion scope)

  • AWS S3 buckets (priority scope).
  • Future extensibility: AWS data services (RDS, Redshift, etc.) as an optional future phase.

Users and endpoint coverage

  • Coverage expansion to 750 PayNet staff.
  • Monitor user data interaction behaviour.
  • Integrate with identity systems (e.g. Entra ID).

3. Data Protection Capabilities

Data residency and boundaries

  • No raw file content leaves the PayNet environment.
  • Only metadata and redacted embeddings are allowed to transit.
  • Deployable as a Private Scan Manager inside PayNet's AWS environment.

Encryption and lifecycle controls

  • Encryption in transit (TLS).
  • Encryption at rest (AES-256).
  • Data masking and redaction for sensitive fields.
  • Secure deletion processes.
  • Audit trail of data scanning and classification.

4. Compliance and Regulatory Requirements

External obligations

  • PDPA (Malaysia): data protection, retention, and breach risk visibility.
  • BNM RMiT and supervisory expectations: visibility and control over sensitive data in cloud.
  • Must support one of the action plans committed for the BNM supervisory letter.

Internal policies

  • Data Classification Policy.
  • Data Protection Standard.
  • Information Security Policy.

5. Reporting Requirements

Operational reporting

  • Sensitive data inventory dashboards.
  • Exposure risk dashboards.
  • High-risk data locations (e.g. public S3 objects).

Management and audit reporting

  • Trend analysis (data exposure reduction over time).
  • Compliance posture summary.
  • Incident and alert reports.

Granularity

  • File-level and dataset-level insights.
  • User-level activity insights for GenAI monitoring.

6. Integration Requirements

Core platforms

  • Microsoft 365 via Graph API.
  • AWS environment via IAM role-based access.

Security ecosystem

  • SIEM (e.g. Devo) via API or logs.
  • Microsoft Purview / MIP for classification alignment.

Identity and access

  • Entra ID / Okta integration.

Recommendation

Recommended six-screen demo backbone for AccuKnox DSPM

1. Connector and scan readiness

Show what is connected, what is still dark, how long first value takes, and which data systems are in scope by cloud and business domain.

2. Sensitive data posture cockpit

Summarize crown-jewel assets, regulatory coverage, risk trend, open critical findings, and business-unit breakdowns in a single landing surface.

3. Exposure and access graph

Visualize who can access which sensitive assets, why that path exists, whether the path is external or over-privileged, and which identities matter most.

4. Investigation workbench

Allow analysts to pivot from one finding into lineage, asset metadata, exposure path, related identities, similar assets, and remediation choices.

5. RBI, DPDP, and TRAI compliance cockpit

Map controls to obligations, show pass or fail status, and generate role-specific evidence packages with timestamps and owner attestations.

6. Remediation command center

Queue fixes, approvals, automation actions, exceptions, SLA breaches, and post-remediation evidence in one operational console.

If these six screens are polished, screenshot-rich, and internally instrumented, AccuKnox can narrow the perception gap with category leaders faster than by adding another long capability checklist.

AccuKnox

Positioning: AI-powered DSPM with agentless deployment, multi-cloud data visibility, and natural adjacency to CNAPP and runtime posture.

Buyer it resonates with: Platform security teams that already believe runtime and cloud posture should inform data-risk decisions.

Most visible product cue: Public pages explain the workflow from inventory to remediation, but the visual layer remains mostly conceptual.
